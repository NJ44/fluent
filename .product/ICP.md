# Fluent — Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)

## Primary ICP: The Functional Adult Who Stutters

### Who They Are

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Who** | Adults who stutter (PWS — People Who Stutter) |
| **Age** | 22–45 primary. Teens and older adults secondary. |
| **Gender** | ~4:1 male:female (stuttering prevalence is skewed male) |
| **Geography** | English-speaking markets first: US, UK, Canada, Australia, Israel |
| **Employment** | Employed or job-seeking. Has routine adult responsibilities (appointments, services, customer service) |
| **Tech comfort** | Uses smartphones daily. Not a techie but comfortable with web apps. |
| **Relationship to stuttering** | Accepted (they know they stutter, they've lived with it), but actively avoids phone calls because of it |

### Their Core Pain

**The call they keep putting off.**

Not emergency situations — those force action. The everyday friction calls:
- Booking a dentist appointment
- Calling the landlord about a repair
- Asking a restaurant if they can accommodate a dietary restriction
- Calling their insurance company
- Ordering food for delivery when the app doesn't work
- Following up on a job application

These stack up. The voicemail box fills. The appointment doesn't get made. The repair takes 3 weeks instead of 3 days.

### Psychographic Profile

**They are not helpless.** They are competent, capable people who are fully articulate in text, often excellent writers, frequently in high-skilled jobs. The stutter is not their entire identity — but the phone is a daily reminder of what they can't do fluently.

**They have tried everything:**
- Practiced calls in advance (rehearsed scripts)
- Asked others to call for them (spouse, friend, assistant)
- Used email and text when possible
- Delayed calls until "I'm in a good speech day"
- Used speech therapy (which helps in therapy, less so on calls to strangers)

**What they want is normalcy** — to just deal with it, the way other people do. Not a workaround they have to explain. Not something that marks them as "different." Ideally something invisible to the person on the other end.

### Emotional Triggers

- "I've been putting off this call for 3 weeks"
- "My friend just called for me and I felt embarrassed"
- "I got flustered on the phone at work and I'm still thinking about it"
- "I can't make this appointment because I don't want to call"
- "I blocked so hard I had to hang up"

### Fears About a Product Like This

1. **"What if it sounds like a robot?"** — voice cloning must pass the human test
2. **"What if they find out it's AI?"** — TCPA disclosure is built in, but the emotional worry remains
3. **"What if the AI says the wrong thing?"** — what-if fallback rules exist to address this
4. **"Is my voice being used without my control?"** — consent passphrase, voice is private, deletable
5. **"This is admitting defeat / I should be able to do this myself"** — positioning must be empowering not compensatory

### Discovery Channels

Where ICP lives online:
- **r/Stutter** (Reddit) — large, active, emotionally candid community
- **Stuttering Foundation forums** — older demographic, institutional community
- **National Stuttering Association (NSA)** — US-based, has annual conference
- **LinkedIn** — professionals who stutter often share their experiences, especially after positive moments
- **YouTube** — "day in the life of someone who stutters" content; creators with large followings

### Triggering Event (When They're Ready to Pay)

The moment they most need Fluent:
- Just had a bad call experience and the shame is fresh
- Has an important call coming up and is already dreading it
- Just got a voicemail and needs to call back
- Is starting a new job and knows phone calls will be required

### Willingness to Pay

- **Free plan** converts easily — no-brainer try-it
- **$19/mo Premium** is reasonable for someone who avoids calls weekly. One good call saves more than the monthly cost.
- **$49/mo Pro** for heavy users (entrepreneurs, sales, service businesses, therapists who stutter, etc.)

## Secondary ICP: Parent of a Child Who Stutters

- Uses Fluent to make calls **on behalf of their child** (doctor appointments, school, etc.)
- More likely to be cost-sensitive
- Not the primary ICP for v1, but worth noting for future positioning

## Tertiary: Severe Social Anxiety (Non-Stutter)

- Phone anxiety without stutter is extremely common (Gen Z particularly)
- Fluent's product works for them but the brand/positioning is stutter-specific
- If validated, a separate positioning track could be explored post-v1
